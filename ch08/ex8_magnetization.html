Magnetization and Permeability

Every atom carries small magnetic moments: its orbiting electrons, the electron spins and (very weakly) the nucleus. Each is a tiny loop of bound (Amperian) current, a dipole m = IbdS. How the moments respond to an applied field sorts materials into the classes of Table 8.1 (Hayt Section 8.5). The dipole moment per unit volume is the magnetization M (A/m); where the loops do not cancel, a net bound current flows, IB = ∮M · dL. Separating it from the free current defines H = B/μ0 − M, so that ∮H · dL = I counts free current only; in a linear medium M = χmH and B = μH (Hayt Section 8.6). Computed values

μ0 = 4π × 10⁻⁷ H/m. χm (magnetic susceptibility) and μr (relative permeability) have no unit; μ (permeability) is in H/m; H and M are in A/m, B in T. The electron spin moment is about ±9 × 10⁻²⁴ A·m² (the Bohr magneton, 9.27 × 10⁻²⁴ A·m²); only electrons in unfilled shells contribute. Antiferromagnetic (NiO, FeS, CoCl2, at low temperatures only) and superparamagnetic materials (ferromagnetic particles in a nonmagnetic matrix, as on recording tape) are in Table 8.1 but not in the picture.
