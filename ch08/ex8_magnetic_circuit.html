The Magnetic Circuit

A magnetic circuit is solved like a resistive one: the coil’s magnetomotive force NI (ampere-turns, A·t) drives the flux Φ through the reluctances ℜ = d/(μS) of the core and the air gap in series, and ∮H · dL = NI says the mmf drops add up to NI. Steel has a curved B–H characteristic, so the circuit is nonlinear; a short air gap takes most of the mmf, stores most of the energy, and pulls its pole faces together with F = B2S/(2μ0) (Hayt Sections 8.8 and 8.9).
