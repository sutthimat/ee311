From the Circuit Symbol to the Fields Inside

In circuit theory C and L are numbers given in the problem. Field theory derives those numbers from geometry and materials. Change the dimensions and the material and watch C and L respond.

(a) Parallel-plate capacitor

\( C=\dfrac{\epsilon_r\epsilon_0 A}{d},\quad Q=CV,\quad E=\dfrac{V}{d},\quad \rho_{s,b}=\Big(1-\dfrac{1}{\epsilon_r}\Big)\rho_s,\quad W_E=\tfrac12 CV^2 \)

Fringing at the plate edges is neglected, which is accurate when d ≪ √A.

(b) Solenoid inductor

\( L\approx\dfrac{\mu_r\mu_0 N^2 A}{l},\quad B\approx\dfrac{\mu_r\mu_0 N I}{l},\quad L_{\text{air}}=K_N\,\dfrac{\mu_0 N^2 A}{l},\quad W_H=\tfrac12 LI^2 \)

The long-solenoid formula assumes all flux stays inside. KN is Nagaoka’s coefficient for a finite air-core solenoid. With an open-ended rod core the real inductance is far below μr times the air value, because the flux must return through air (see Topic 4).
