A Coaxial Cable, Cut Back

An RG-58 coaxial cable (Belden 8240) with its layers cut back one by one, drawn to scale from the manufacturer's datasheet: the solid copper inner conductor, the polyethylene insulation, the braided shield and the PVC jacket. With a voltage between the inner conductor and the shield, the electric field E points radially across the insulation; with a current out on the inner conductor and back on the shield, the magnetic field H circles the inner conductor. Turn the cable to look at it from any side.

Formula values use the datasheet radii a = 0.419 mm and b = 1.475 mm and an assumed εr = 2.25 (a typical value for polyethylene; the datasheet does not give it): C′ = 2πε/ln(b/a) (Hayt Ch. 6), L′ = (μ0/2π) ln(b/a) (external inductance, Hayt Ch. 8), v = 1/√(L′C′). The datasheet values are the manufacturer's nominal values. The formula capacitance is about 6 % above the datasheet value. The formulas describe an idealised cable (smooth, perfectly round conductors and a uniform insulation of the assumed permittivity), so a difference of this size is to be expected; the datasheet values are the ones to quote.
