Divergence and Maxwell's First Equation

The divergence of D is the net outflow of flux from a small closed surface per unit volume, as the volume shrinks to zero: div D = limΔv→0 ∮ D·dS/Δv (Hayt §3.5). It is a scalar, and it equals the volume charge density: div D = ρv, Maxwell's first equation. Drag the small probe box through a field: its faces show where flux leaves and where it enters, and the box shows the net outflow per volume. Equations used

div D = limΔv→0 ∮S D·dS/Δv   (11)
div D = ∂Dx/∂x + ∂Dy/∂y + ∂Dz/∂z   (12)
div D = (1/ρ) ∂(ρDρ)/∂ρ + (1/ρ) ∂Dφ/∂φ + ∂Dz/∂z   (13)
div D = (1/r²) ∂(r²Dr)/∂r + (1/(r sin θ)) ∂(sin θ Dθ)/∂θ + (1/(r sin θ)) ∂Dφ/∂φ   (14)
div D = ρv   (15), Maxwell's first equation

The divergence of a vector is a scalar: no unit vectors in the answer. D in C/m² gives div D in C/m³. Angles are given in degrees; in (13) and (14) they enter only through sin and cos (the derivatives are taken with the angle in radians, which the formulas already allow for).
