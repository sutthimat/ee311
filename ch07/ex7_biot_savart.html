The Biot–Savart Law

A steady current is split into elements I dL. Each element sets up a field dH at a point, normal to the plane of dL and R (right-handed screw from dL to R), proportional to I dL sin θ and falling off as 1/R²; the field of the whole circuit is the sum (Hayt Section 7.1). For a straight filament the sum gives H = (I/4πρ)(sin α2 − sin α1)aφ, and I/(2πρ) for an infinite one. Pick a mode: Explore, the slides’ examples and drills step by step, or a random drill.

Equations used

(1)  dH = I dL × aR/(4πR²) = I dL × R/(4πR³)
(2)  dH2 = I1 dL1 × aR12/(4πR12²)
(3)  H = ∮ I dL × aR/(4πR²)
(4)  I = ∫ K dN;  (5)  I dL = K dS = J dv
(6)  H = ∫S K × aR dS/(4πR²);  (7)  H = ∫vol J × aR dv/(4πR²)
(8)  H = I/(2πρ) aφ  (infinite straight filament)
(9)  H = I/(4πρ) (sin α2 − sin α1) aφ  (finite filament)
α1, α2: at the field point, from the perpendicular to the filament line to its two ends, positive toward the current; μ0 = 4π × 10⁻⁷ H/m, B = μ0H
