Ampère’s Circuital Law

The line integral of H about any closed path equals the direct current enclosed by the path, ∮H · dL = I (10). The current enclosed is the current that pierces a surface spanning the path, like a rubber sheet stretched over it; it counts as positive when the fingers of the right hand follow the path and the thumb points along the current. Where symmetry makes |H| constant along a circle, the law gives H at once: a filament, a round conductor, a coaxial cable (Hayt Section 7.2). Equations used

(10)  ∮H · dL = I  (the current enclosed by the path)
(8)  H = I/(2πρ) aφ  (infinite filament)
Uniform J in a round wire of radius a: Ienc = I ρ²/a² for ρ < a
Coax, ρ < a: Hφ = Iρ/(2πa²);  a < ρ < b: Hφ = I/(2πρ)
Coax, b < ρ < c: Hφ = (I/2πρ)(c² − ρ²)/(c² − b²);  ρ > c: Hφ = 0
Filament off the axis: H = I/(2πρ) aφ, aφ = az × aρ, aρ from the filament to the point
