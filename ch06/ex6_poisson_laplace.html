Poisson’s and Laplace’s Equations

From ∇·D = ρv, D = εE and E = −∇V follows Poisson’s equation ∇²V = −ρv/ε, and where there is no volume charge, Laplace’s equation ∇²V = 0 (Hayt Section 6.6). The Laplacian ∇²V compares V at a point with the average of V around it: on a grid of spacing h, ∇²V ≈ (V1 + V2 + V3 + V4 − 4V(P))/h². So in a charge-free region V at a point is the average of its neighbours, and the potential has no peak or dip inside; a positive volume charge makes a peak. Equations used

∇·D = ρv   (21),   D = εE   (22),   E = −∇V   (23)
∇·∇V = −ρv/ε   (24)   (ε constant)
∇²V = ∂²V/∂x² + ∂²V/∂y² + ∂²V/∂z² = −ρv/ε   (25), (26)   Poisson
∇²V = 0   (27)   Laplace (no volume charge)
∇²V = (1/ρ) ∂/∂ρ(ρ ∂V/∂ρ) + (1/ρ²) ∂²V/∂φ² + ∂²V/∂z²   (29)
∇²V = (1/r²) ∂/∂r(r² ∂V/∂r) + (1/(r² sin θ)) ∂/∂θ(sin θ ∂V/∂θ) + (1/(r² sin² θ)) ∂²V/∂φ²   (30)
∇²V ≈ (V1 + V2 + V3 + V4 − 4V(P))/h²   (four neighbours at ±h in x and y; V independent of z)
ρv = −ε0∇²V   (free space, ε0 = 8.854 × 10⁻¹² F/m)

(28) is (26) with ρv = 0. The terms of (28), (29) and (30) at a point differ from one system to another, but their sum, ∇²V, is the same scalar. Angles are given in degrees; the derivatives are taken with angles in radians, as (29) and (30) assume.
