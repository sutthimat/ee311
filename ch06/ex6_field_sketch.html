Curvilinear Squares: Capacitance from a Field Sketch

In a two-dimensional problem the conductor surfaces are equipotentials, and the flux lines leave them at right angles and cross every equipotential at 90°. Divide the potential difference into NV equal steps ΔV, and start the flux lines so that each flux tube carries the same flux ΔΨ = εΔV: then every cell of the map is a curvilinear square, ΔLt/ΔLN = 1, and counting the tubes gives the capacitance per metre, C = εNQ/NV (Hayt Section 6.5, Eqs. (18)–(20)). The map here is computed (finite differences), not sketched; a careful hand sketch reaches 5–10 percent. Equations used

E = ΔΨ/(ε ΔLt) and E = ΔV/ΔLN: (1/ε)(ΔΨ/ΔLt) = ΔV/ΔLN   (18)
ΔLt/ΔLN = (1/ε)(ΔΨ/ΔV) = constant   (19); ΔΨ = εΔV gives 1: curvilinear squares
Q = NQ ΔΨ, V0 = NV ΔV: C = ε NQ/NV (per metre)   (20)
Coax (exact): C = 2πε/ln(b/a)   (5), per metre
Two cylinders, radii R and r, axes d apart (exact, as in Section 6.4): C = 2πε/cosh⁻¹[(R² + r² − d²)/(2Rr)]
