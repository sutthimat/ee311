Capacitance of a Two-Wire Line

Two parallel line charges +ρL at x = a and −ρL at x = −a have circular equipotentials, but the circles are not centred on the line charges. Any one of them may be replaced by a conducting cylinder of radius b whose axis is at h from the plane x = 0, which is the surface V = 0. That gives the capacitance of a cylinder over a conducting plane, C = 2πεL/cosh⁻¹(h/b), and of the two-wire line, half as much (Hayt Section 6.4). Equations used

One line charge: V = (ρL/2πε) ln(R0/R)
(11)  V = (ρL/4πε) ln{[(x + a)² + y²]/[(x − a)² + y²]}
(12)  K1 = exp(4πεV1/ρL);  circle: h = a(K1 + 1)/(K1 − 1),  b = 2a√K1/(K1 − 1)
(13)  a = √(h² − b²);  (14)  √K1 = [h + √(h² − b²)]/b;  (15)  ρL = 4πεV0/ln K1
(16)  C = 2πεL/ln{[h + √(h² − b²)]/b} = 2πεL/cosh⁻¹(h/b)
(17)  C ≈ 2πεL/ln(2h/b)  (b ≪ h)
Two-wire line, axes 2h apart: C = πεL/cosh⁻¹(h/b), half of (16)
On the cylinder: ρS = DN = ρLa/[2πb(h + b cos φ)]  (from (11); max at φ = 180°)
