The Laplacian

The Laplacian ∇²f compares the value of f at a point with the average of f around it. On a small circle of radius r about P, the average is about f(P) + (r²/4)∇²f: a positive Laplacian means P sits in a dip (its surroundings are higher), a negative one means P sits on a bump. Definition

∇²f = ∇·∇f = ∂²f/∂x² + ∂²f/∂y² + ∂²f/∂z²

The Laplacian is the divergence of the gradient, a scalar. All surfaces here depend on x and y only, so ∂²f/∂z² = 0 and ∇²f = ∂²f/∂x² + ∂²f/∂y².

Average on a small circle

average of f on a circle of radius r ≈ f(P) + (r²/4) ∇²f    so  ∇²f ≈ (4/r²)(average − f(P))

From the Taylor series of f about P: the first-order terms and the mixed term average to zero around the circle, and the averages of (x − xP)² and (y − yP)² are each r²/2. The terms left out are of order r⁴, so the estimate of ∇²f is off by a term of order r². In three dimensions the same idea uses a small sphere: average ≈ f(P) + (r²/6)∇²f.

Laplace's and Poisson's equations

∇²V = −ρv/ε  (Poisson)     ∇²V = 0  where ρv = 0  (Laplace)

Where there is no charge, V at every point equals the average of its surroundings, so V can have no local maximum or minimum there: the extreme values sit on the boundary (try the saddle and Relax). Charge bends the potential: positive ρv gives ∇²V < 0, a bump (try the charged slab).
