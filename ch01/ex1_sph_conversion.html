Spherical Coordinates: Converting Points and Vectors

A point P is located by its distance r from the origin, the angle θ from the +z axis (0° to 180°) and the angle φ from the +x axis, the same φ as in cylindrical coordinates. At P the unit vectors ar (outward), aθ (“south”) and aφ (“east”) form a right-handed set. A vector at P is resolved into them by dot products: Ar = A·ar, and so on, with Table 1.2 (Hayt §1.9). x = r sin θ cos φ,  y = r sin θ sin φ,  z = r cos θ
r = √(x² + y² + z²),  θ = cos⁻¹(z/r),  φ = tan⁻¹(y/x)

r ≥ 0 and 0° ≤ θ ≤ 180°. A calculator's tan⁻¹ gives −90° to 90° only: when x < 0, add 180° (if y > 0) or subtract 180° (if y < 0) to put φ in the quadrant of (x, y).

araθaφ

Each component of A = Ax ax + Ay ay + Az az is one column of the table: Ar = Ax(ax·ar) + Ay(ay·ar) + Az(az·ar), and the same for Aθ and Aφ. The unit vectors turn from point to point, so even a constant vector such as 10 ax has different spherical components at different points.
