Divergence

The divergence of D at a point is the net outward flux of D through a small closed surface around the point, per unit volume, as the volume shrinks to zero. Here the surface is a cube of side Δ. Each face shows the flux through it: outward (solid orange, +) or inward (dashed navy, −). The page opens on a uniformly charged ball with the cube across its surface: press Shrink and the ratio settles on the charge density at the centre. Drag the figure to turn it; drag the black centre dot to move the cube.

Definition and results

∇·D = limΔv→0 (∮S D·dS) / Δv    (C/m³)
∇·D = ∂Dx/∂x + ∂Dy/∂y + ∂Dz/∂z
Maxwell's first equation (point form of Gauss's law):   ∇·D = ρv

Each face flux is the surface integral of D·dS over the face: numerical (adaptive 4 × 4-point Gauss–Legendre) for the smooth fields and the ball; in closed form (the solid angle of the face seen from the charge) for the point charge, whose field is too sharp near the charge for a fixed rule. All values to 3 significant figures; the highlighted row is the present Δ.

Reading the numbers

By Gauss's law the net flux equals the charge enclosed, so ∮ D·dS / Δv is the average charge density inside the cube. As Δ → 0 that average becomes the density at the centre, which is ∇·D = ρv.
