Distance and Unit Vectors

The vector from P to Q is the difference of their position vectors, RPQ = rQ − rP. Its components are the three steps along x, y and z that lead from P to Q, its magnitude |RPQ| is the distance from P to Q, and aPQ = RPQ/|RPQ| is the unit vector in its direction, which ends on the sphere of radius 1 about P (Hayt §1.4). Equations used

rP = xP ax + yP ay + zP az
RPQ = rQ − rP
|B| = √(Bx² + By² + Bz²)
aB = B / |B|
RPQ = (xQ − xP) ax + (yQ − yP) ay + (zQ − zP) az

Reversing the order reverses the vector, RQP = −RPQ, with the same distance. A vector may be slid parallel to itself (for example to the origin) without changing its components. A lowercase a with a subscript is always a unit vector: its magnitude is 1 and it carries direction only.
