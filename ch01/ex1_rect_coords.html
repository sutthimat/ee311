Points and the Differential Box

In rectangular coordinates a point P(x, y, z) is the one point where three planes meet: x = constant, y = constant and z = constant (Hayt §1.3). The axes are right-handed: turning the x axis into the y axis advances a right-handed screw along +z. Increasing each coordinate by dx, dy, dz leads to P′(x + dx, y + dy, z + dz); the six planes through P and P′ enclose a box with volume dv = dx dy dz, faces dx dy, dy dz, dz dx and diagonal dL. Right-handed axes: curl the fingers of the right hand from x toward y (through 90°) and the thumb points along +z. Each face area appears twice on the box (opposite faces are equal); its face is normal to the axis that is not in its name, so dy dz is normal to ax. In the integrals of the later chapters dx, dy, dz → 0: the box here is drawn large only so that it can be seen.
