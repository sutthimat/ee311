Curl

Curl measures how much a field circulates around a point. Take a small closed loop at P with unit normal aN, add up H·dL around it, and divide by the area ΔS; as the loop shrinks to the point, this ratio is the component (∇×H)·aN. Drag P (the ringed dot), tilt the loop, shrink it, or press Play to turn a paddle wheel whose spin follows the curl. Drag elsewhere on the figure to turn the view.

Play turns the paddle wheel at ω = ½ (∇×H)·aN rad/s per A/m², as a small paddle wheel would turn in a fluid whose velocity (m/s) equals H (A/m); real time, counter-clockwise seen from the tip of aN when positive.

Definition

(∇×H)·aN = limΔS→0 (∮ H·dL) / ΔS

The loop of area ΔS lies in the plane normal to aN and is traversed counter-clockwise seen from the tip of aN (right-hand rule). The direction of ∇×H is the normal that gives the largest circulation; its magnitude is that largest circulation per unit area.

Cartesian components

∇×H = (∂Hz/∂y − ∂Hy/∂z) ax + (∂Hx/∂z − ∂Hz/∂x) ay + (∂Hy/∂x − ∂Hx/∂y) az
