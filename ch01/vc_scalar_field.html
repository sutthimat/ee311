Scalar Fields

A scalar field assigns one number, with its unit, to every point of a region: a temperature T in °C, a potential V in volts. Here the region is the square −3 m ≤ x ≤ 3 m, −3 m ≤ y ≤ 3 m of the xy-plane. The same field is drawn twice: as a height surface, and as a colour map with level curves (contour lines; equipotential lines when the field is a potential) on the base plane underneath. Drag the probe dot to read the value at any point, and drag elsewhere on the figure to turn it.

Drag the figure to turn it: left–right changes the azimuth, up–down the elevation. Drag the probe dot (on the surface or on the base) to move the probe. Seen from high above, the surface fades so the base map stays readable.

Display

The height is only a picture of the number. The field lives in the plane: one value at each point (x, y), as the colour map shows.
