1 Coulomb’s law and the field of a point charge

The force that point charge \(Q_1\) exerts on point charge \(Q_2\), a distance \(R_{12}\) away, acts along the straight line joining the two charges. Its magnitude is inversely proportional to the square of the distance.

\[ \mathbf F_2=\frac{Q_1Q_2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0\,R_{12}^{\,2}}\,\mathbf a_{12} \]

where \(\mathbf a_{12}\) is the unit vector pointing from \(Q_1\) to \(Q_2\), and \(1/4\pi\varepsilon_0\approx 8.988\times10^{9}\ \mathrm{N\cdot m^2/C^2}\)

The electric field intensity is the force per unit test charge. Its unit is V/m.

\[ \mathbf E=\frac{\mathbf F_t}{Q_t}=\frac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0\,R^{2}}\,\mathbf a_R \]

If charge \(Q_1\) is at position \(\mathbf r_1\) and we want the field at point \(\mathbf r\), the vector from the charge to the observation point is \(\mathbf r-\mathbf r_1\), and \(\mathbf a_R=(\mathbf r-\mathbf r_1)/|\mathbf r-\mathbf r_1|\), so

\[ \mathbf E(\mathbf r)=\frac{Q_1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0\,|\mathbf r-\mathbf r_1|^{2}}\,\frac{\mathbf r-\mathbf r_1}{|\mathbf r-\mathbf r_1|}=\frac{Q_1(\mathbf r-\mathbf r_1)}{4\pi\varepsilon_0\,|\mathbf r-\mathbf r_1|^{3}} \]

A positive charge gives a field pointing away from the charge. A negative charge gives a field pointing toward the charge.

2 Field of several point charges

Coulomb’s law is linear. The force that one charge exerts on a test charge does not depend on whether other charges are present. The total field at point \(P\) is therefore the vector sum of the fields due to the individual charges.

\[ \mathbf E(\mathbf r)=\mathbf E_1+\mathbf E_2=\frac{Q_1(\mathbf r-\mathbf r_1)}{4\pi\varepsilon_0\,|\mathbf r-\mathbf r_1|^{3}}+\frac{Q_2(\mathbf r-\mathbf r_2)}{4\pi\varepsilon_0\,|\mathbf r-\mathbf r_2|^{3}} \]

For \(n\) point charges

\[ \mathbf E(\mathbf r)=\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{Q_m(\mathbf r-\mathbf r_m)}{|\mathbf r-\mathbf r_m|^{3}} \]
