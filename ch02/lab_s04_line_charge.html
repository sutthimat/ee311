Finite vs infinite line charge

Notice: close to a finite line (ρ ≪ L/2) its field matches the infinite-line 1/ρ law; far away (ρ ≫ L) it looks like a point charge Q = ρLL with 1/ρ².

\mathbf E=\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}\frac{\rho_L\,dz'}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{\rho\,\mathbf a_\rho+(z_0-z')\mathbf a_z}{[\rho^2+(z_0-z')^2]^{3/2}} \text{infinite: } E_\rho=\frac{\rho_L}{2\pi\epsilon_0\rho} Finite case computed by adaptive Simpson quadrature. Green band: within 1 % of the infinite line. Drag on the plot to move the probe.
