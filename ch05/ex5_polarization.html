Polarization in Dielectrics

A dielectric is an arrangement of microscopic dipoles p = Qd whose charges are bound: a field only shifts them slightly. Polar molecules have permanent dipoles, randomly oriented until a field aligns them; nonpolar molecules become dipoles only in a field. The dipole moment per unit volume is the polarization P = np (C/m²). Where P crosses a surface, bound charge crosses it, ΔQb = P·ΔS, and the free charge alone is found from D = ε0E + P. In a linear isotropic dielectric P = χeε0E and D = ε0εrE with εr = 1 + χe (Hayt Section 5.7). Pick a mode: Explore, the slide examples step by step, or a random drill.

Equations used

p = Qd   (20)
P = limΔv→0 (1/Δv) Σ pi = np   (21)
ΔQb = P·ΔS,   Qb = −∮S P·dS   (22)
QT = ∮S ε0E·dS   (23)
Q = QT − Qb = ∮S (ε0E + P)·dS   (24)
D = ε0E + P   (25),   Q = ∮S D·dS   (26)
∇·P = −ρb,   ∇·ε0E = ρT,   ∇·D = ρv   (27)
P = χeε0E   (28),   εr = χe + 1   (29)
D = ε0εrE = εE   (30),   ε = ε0εr   (31)

Computed values

ε0 = 8.854 × 10⁻¹² F/m. χe (electric susceptibility) and εr (relative permittivity, the dielectric constant) have no unit; ε (permittivity) is in F/m. In a linear isotropic dielectric E and P are parallel whatever the field direction; in an anisotropic one (a single crystal) ε is a 3 × 3 matrix and D is not parallel to E.
