The Method of Images

A dipole has an infinite plane of zero potential midway between its charges. A thin conducting plane at V = 0 put there leaves the field above it unchanged. Conversely, a charge above a conducting plane may be replaced by the charge and its image, an equal and opposite charge at the mirror point, with the plane removed: the field above the plane is the same, and below it the field is zero (Hayt Section 5.5). The image gives the field without knowing the induced surface charge, and then ρS = DN gives that charge. Pick a mode: Explore, the slides' examples and Drill D5.6 step by step, or a random drill.

Equations used

Image: −Q (or −ρL) at the mirror point; the plane removed; valid only above the plane
Line charge: E = ρL/(2πε0R) aR = (ρL/2πε0) R/R²
With its image: V = (ρL/2πε0) ln(R−/R+), zero on the plane
Point charge with its image: V = (Q/4πε0)(1/R+ − 1/R−)
At the conductor: ρS = DN = ε0EN   (Section 5.4)
Line charge at height h: ρS(x) = −ρLh/[π(x² + h²)], in all −ρL per metre
Point charge at height h: ρS(r) = −Qh/[2π(r² + h²)3/2], in all −Q
