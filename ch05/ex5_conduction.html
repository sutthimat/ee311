Metallic Conductors and Ohm’s Law

In a metal the valence electrons are free to move. A field E gives them a small average drift velocity vd = −μeE, against the field, so the current density is J = −ρeμeE = σE, the point form of Ohm’s law. For a uniform conductor of length L and cross section S this gives V = IR with R = L/(σS) (Hayt Section 5.3). In a semiconductor, electrons and holes both carry current along E (Section 5.6).
