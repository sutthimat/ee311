The Electric Dipole

Two point charges +Q and −Q a distance d apart form an electric dipole (Hayt Section 4.7). With +Q at (0, 0, d/2) and −Q at (0, 0, −d/2), the potential at P is the sum of two scalars, V = (Q/4πε0)(1/R1 − 1/R2). Far away, R2 − R1 ≐ d cos θ and R1R2 ≈ r², so V = Qd cos θ/(4πε0r²) = p·ar/(4πε0r²), with the dipole moment p = Qd from −Q to +Q, and E = −∇V. The dipole's V falls off as 1/r² and its E as 1/r³, one power faster than for a single charge. Pick a mode: Explore, the slides' derivations and drills step by step, or a random drill.
